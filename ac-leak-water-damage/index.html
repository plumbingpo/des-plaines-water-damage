AC Leak Water Damage Help in Des Plaines

Your air conditioner pulls moisture out of the air all day, and that water has to go somewhere. A pan under the coil catches the drips, and from there a thin pipe empties the pan. Once dust and algae block the drain, water backs up until it runs over the edge of the pan. With an air handler in the attic or a hallway closet, the overflow goes straight into the ceiling, wall or floor around it.

People tend to underestimate what a small, steady leak can do. The water wicks into drywall, soaks the platform under the unit and creeps under flooring, often with mold already started by the time a stain shows. Tracking the full extent of the moisture, drying it and making repairs is our part. An HVAC technician still has to fix the unit or unclog the line, and we plan our work around that appointment.

Do This First

What to Do Right Now

  1. Shut the System DownSwitch the system off at the thermostat so it stops making condensate. Where the wiring on the air handler is getting wet, the breaker should also go off.
  2. Keep Clear of Wet ElectricalCeiling lights, outlets and the unit itself are off limits while wet, and stay that way until their circuit has been turned off.
  3. Empty the Drain PanUse a wet-vac or rags to take the standing water out of the pan and stop the spill, provided the pan is safe to reach. Do not crawl across a wet attic to do it.
  4. Soak Up What You CanPress towels into damp carpet, mop hard flooring, put a bucket below any drip from the ceiling, and clear furniture, clothes and boxes out of the closet or away from the stain.
  5. Bring In Two ProsCall us to check how far the water spread, and call an HVAC technician to clear the drain line. Leave the system off until it is fixed.
Why It Happens

What Causes It

  • The drain pipe for condensate blocked by a buildup of dust and algae.
  • Water escaping from a drain pan that has cracked, rusted through or tilted.
  • No working float switch to stop the system when the pan filled.
  • Loose or separated fittings on the condensate pipe that drip where nobody can see.
  • A frozen evaporator coil that thawed faster than the pan could drain.
If It Is Left

Where the Damage Spreads

The Platform and Closet Walls

In a closet, the unit usually rests on a wooden stand built above the return air box. Leaks decay the stand and climb the closet drywall, concealed by the equipment itself.

Baseboards and Door Trim

Baseboards and door casings near the closet draw water up from the floor. Because pressed-wood trim puffs up and falls apart when wet, new material goes in.

Mold Around the Air Handler

Damp drywall and wood beside the air handler can grow mold within 24 to 48 hours, and the return air can pull spores into the ductwork and through the house.

Attic Insulation

Insulation around an attic unit soaks up overflow and holds it against the ceiling. Soaked sections usually come out, since they no longer insulate.

How We Fix It

Our AC Leak Water Damage Process

1

Find Every Wet Area

We use moisture meters and thermal imaging around the air handler, the ceiling or walls next to it, and the flooring outside the closet to find every wet material, not only the stained ones.

2

Extract and Open Up

We extract the water sitting in the carpet, the platform and the pan. Saturated or moldy insulation and drywall are removed, which opens the cavity to dry air.

3

Set Drying Equipment

Air movers and dehumidifiers run for several days, usually 3 to 5, while we track the readings. Access to the air handler is kept open for your HVAC technician.

4

Clean and Restore

Since condensate left standing for days is seldom clean, we apply an antimicrobial to the surfaces it touched. New flooring, baseboard, drywall and insulation follow, and fresh paint makes the area match.

Will Insurance Pay?

What About Insurance?

Timing is usually what decides whether an AC leak is covered. Water damage is paid for under many homeowners policies when the overflow was sudden and you responded as soon as you discovered it. If the adjuster decides the drip went on for weeks or months, it may be treated as a gradual leak or a maintenance issue and denied. Either way, the cost of fixing the AC unit is typically left out. Read your policy, and ask us for photos and moisture readings to support the claim.
